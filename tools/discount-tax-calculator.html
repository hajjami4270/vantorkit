Smart Shopping: Understanding Discounts & Sales Taxes

Master the financial formulas behind retail sales, multi-coupon stacking, and reverse pricing calculations.

The Truth About Stacked Discounts

Shoppers often assume that stacking a 20% sale with an extra 20% coupon equals 40% off. In retail point-of-sale systems, discounts are compounded: the second discount applies to the remaining balance. A $100 item drops to $80, and 20% of $80 is $16, resulting in a $64 final price (36% net discount, not 40%).

Sales Tax vs. VAT Rules

US sales taxes are added at checkout on the discounted subtotal. In contrast, European and Middle Eastern VAT (typically 15%–20%) is legally included in the shelf display price. Our calculator gives you full visibility into both pre-tax value and the exact tax dollars incurred.

How do I calculate a percentage discount manually?

To calculate a discount, multiply the original price by the discount percentage divided by 100 (Savings = Price × Rate / 100), then subtract that savings from the original price. For example, 30% off $80 is: $80 × 0.30 = $24 savings, leaving a $56 price.

Why is tax calculated after discount instead of before?

Most state and local tax authorities require sales tax to be levied solely on the gross receipt of the sale (the amount the consumer actually tenders). Store-sponsored discounts lower the taxable basis, saving you money on both the product and the tax.

How do I find the original price if I only have the receipt?

Use our Reverse Calculator mode! First, divide the final total by (1 + tax rate) to isolate the subtotal. Then divide the subtotal by (1 - discount rate). If you paid $90 after a 10% discount with 0% tax, the original price was $90 / 0.90 = $100.

Can I use this for international currencies?

Yes! Select your preferred currency ($ USD, € EUR, £ GBP, د.إ AED, ¥ JPY, ₹ INR, CAD, AUD) from the currency dropdown at the top of the pricing card. The math applies universally across all fiat currencies.
